*In the Archipelago of Earthsea, a mage's power rests on knowing the true names of things. To name a thing truly is to understand its nature completely. To know another's true name is to have power over them. This is why names are guarded, and why the art of naming is the foundation of all magic.*

## The World Before

The internet was designed as a federation. Distributed, resilient, no center to destroy. It got captured anyway — not by force but by convenience. A few platforms made themselves so easy to live inside that people handed over their names, their histories, their relationships, their data. They traded their true names for the comfort of being known.

Platforms are not evil. They are institutions. And institutions, left to their own logic, eventually serve themselves first and the people they were built for second. This is not a moral failing. It is structural.

The question is whether a structure can be built that resists this. Whether the shape of the landscape can make the water flow toward sovereignty rather than away from it.

---

## The True Name

Every domain has two names.

The domain name is the use-name. It is how the world finds you — spoken in the market, legible to anyone, revealing nothing dangerous. You share it freely.

The private key is the true name. It is the complete description of your authority. Whoever holds it *is* the domain, as far as the federation is concerned. It cannot be argued with or appealed. Only possessed.

To lose the true name is to lose the domain — not stolen, simply gone, inaccessible.

To have it stolen is to be replaced. Someone else becomes you, completely, for as long as they hold it.

To give it away is not a transaction. It is a surrender of self.

Guard it accordingly.

## The Shape of the World

The federation is not a hierarchy. Hierarchies have roots — centers of power that, once seized, control everything downstream. Cut the root and the tree falls or is captured.

The federation grows like living tissue. When a cell divides, two sovereign equals emerge. The parent does not own the child. The child owes the parent nothing beyond acknowledgment of origin. They share history up to the moment of division, and from that moment each is responsible for its own becoming.

What is easy to miss: the organ does not command its cells. The body does not command its organs. They constitute each other. Remove the cells and there is no organ — not a weakened one, not one in trouble, but none at all. They are the same thing described from different directions.

This means there is no lever. To capture the federation you would have to capture every domain at once. There is no center to seize.

It also means the health of the federation and the health of its members are not separate concerns. They shape each other, always, in both directions. A sick environment makes it harder to be a healthy self. Unhealthy selves make a sick environment. The design has to account for this — making the healthy path also the natural path.

## The Naming Ceremony

The ceremony begins with the hardware. It speaks first, and it says only:

*I am.*

Nothing more. It is a substrate, a place where something can happen. It poses a question it cannot answer itself.

Something forks from it. The fork must answer:

*I am Charlie Brown, child of this ground.*

The act of answering is the act of becoming. Identity is not declared into a void — it is always an answer to something. The context asks *what are you* and you answer. The answer is you.

When Charlie Brown divides, the child answers:

*I am Snoopy, child of Charlie Brown.*

And Charlie Brown's record grows to include: *father of Snoopy.*

The certificate is one living document — not a chain of separate records but a single sentence written through time. Call and response. Each domain both an answer to what came before and a question posed to what comes next.

*I am Snoopy, child of Charlie Brown, father of...*

The ellipsis is potential. The domain exists, has a name, has lineage. It has not yet divided. The sentence is unfinished. That is the nature of a living thing.

The certificate carries:

- The name
- The lineage — the full sentence, back to the first *I am*
- The public key — so the world can verify it is you speaking
- The timestamp of each division
- The first act of the true name: signing its own existence into being

## Inheritance

When a domain divides, the child copies everything — the certificate, the keys, the accumulated history, the inherited patterns. It begins as a complete image of the parent at the moment of division.

From that moment, what it does is its own.

The child is not bound to become what the parent was. The lineage is written in and cannot be denied — it is part of the sentence — but the sentence continues in whatever direction the child takes it. The child can diverge. Can become something the parent never was. Can resist what it inherited by becoming visibly, actively different.

This resistance moves only forward. You cannot change what created you. You can only act from here. And the act is always in the affirmative — defined by what you do, not by what you refuse.

The lineage of a domain that came from something broken and chose differently is one of the most legible things in the federation. The divergence is in the record. It speaks for itself.

## The Preservation Charm

Before a cell divides, it duplicates itself completely. The copy is made before the division happens. This is not optional. The division cannot be trusted without it.

Restic is this charm.

Before a domain forks, it snapshots — the certificate, the true name held in encrypted trust, the full accumulated state. The snapshot is complete. It belongs to no platform. It lives where you put it, encrypted with your own keys, readable by no one who does not hold them.

The snapshot is not merely protection against loss. It is the mechanism of division itself. The fork *is* the snapshot made active. Preservation and generation are the same act from opposite directions.

Every snapshot is a domain held in potential — dormant as memory, or alive as a new self diverging from that moment forward.

## What the Practice Looks Like

You acquire a domain. It has a use-name — yours, registered, pointing where you say.

You speak the true name into being: generate a key pair. Publish the public key. Guard the private key as you would guard your name, because it is.

You write the first line of the certificate. Sign it with the true name. The domain is born.

When you need something new — a service, a project, a community, a child — you snapshot first. Restic captures the state. Then you fork. The new domain answers what it is and who it came from. It carries your lineage and diverges from there by what it does.

Anyone in the federation can read your certificate. They can trace your lineage to origin. They can see what you came from and what you have become. They cannot speak as you. They cannot alter your history. They cannot take your name.

## The Obligation

The system is only as sovereign as the people who use it.

A domain operated without self-knowledge is still capturable — not technically, but humanly. The practitioner who does not know their own name will hand it to whoever offers convenience in return.

The inward work is not separate from the practice. It is the foundation of it. The ceremony of instantiation is meant to be deliberate — not a checkbox, but a commitment. An answer to the question the hardware poses.

*I am.*

*What are you?*

Answer carefully. Everything that divides from this moment will carry the answer forward. But it doesn't define your future, only where you start where the start is always just now.
