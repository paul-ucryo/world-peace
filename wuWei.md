# Wu Wei & Nudge

Oct 18, 2026 · @paul

## Philosophy of Habit

Wu wei and nudge are both systems of psychology — each describing how to use your environment efficiently to achieve your goals. But without a coherent framework of action, they remain largely inaccessible. Treating the practice like exercise, like physical therapy, gives us that framework. The system learns to leverage its environment toward its goals, gradually blurring the boundary between self and other. The universal principle is simple: picture the world you want, identify the lowest hanging fruit toward that goal, and do that.

## The Practice

Walk into a room. Notice what could be slightly better. Pick the easiest thing that moves in the right direction. Do it. Move to the next room.

Like working out, the capacity scales with use. Early on you're just noticing the list exists. Then you read it faster. Then you develop judgment about effort-to-return ratios. You start doing two things with the effort one used to cost. The progress is slow and never feels completely. But slowly you feel stronger and more competent and generally more healthy. It always feels hard, but the practice makes that addictive.&#32;

## Compounding and Scale

The effects compound outward in ways you cannot track or take credit for. You're always only reducing friction immediately around you — but systems are connected. Ease propagates. Someone you made things easier for makes things easier for someone else. The chain runs further than you can see.

You don't need to reach the far end of the problem. You need to keep going in your immediate vicinity, consistently, at low cost, without burning out. The cumulative effect of many people doing this — uncoordinated, unremarkable, persistent — is how most lasting change has actually worked. Making the lives of people around you easier makes it easier for those people to support a family member in a domestic violence situation — something profound you couldn't have imagined. And they are only able to provide that support because of how easy you make your interactions with them.

## When the System Resists

The practice isn't about getting the system to bend to your will. It is about changing the conditions of your surroundings to align with your values. And if you are consistent, and those values are valuable, the system will adopt them — making it easier for you to express a little more. And if the system continues to resist you, your best option is still to make your surroundings as comfortable as possible. Doing that one small thing sets it up for the next person, even if that person ends up always being you. Self-love is important, and it should be an expected part of your practice.
