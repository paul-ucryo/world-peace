# Resolution and Routing: The Two Domains of Governance

## The Two Functions

Any system that organizes collective effort operates across two domains.

**Problem resolution** is the work of engaging with reality — identifying what is wrong, understanding why, closing the gap between what is and what should be. It is expensive, slow, and resistant. Reality pushes back. Progress is often ambiguous. The feedback loop is functional: did the problem get better?

**Routing** is the work of moving signal — coordinating attention, building coalition, situating local problems within larger contexts, making information legible at scale. Without it, even correct solutions cannot be implemented. A system that cannot route cannot organize itself beyond the immediate. The feedback loop is relational: did the signal move?

Both are necessary. Neither is sufficient alone.

---

## What Happens at the Extremes

The two domains make competing demands on the same finite energy budget. A system that optimizes entirely for one at the expense of the other becomes pathological in a specific and predictable way.

**Optimize entirely for resolution** and you get gang law.

This is not incompetence. Gang law resolves problems with real efficiency within its domain. The hierarchy works, the rules are enforced, the violence is functional. But the resolution capacity has no altitude — it cannot see beyond the immediate, cannot model consequences outside the local, cannot abstract to systemic causes or distant effects.

The consequence of zero altitude is that everything carries maximum weight. Every slight is existential. Every transaction is life and death. Not because the people are irrational, but because without the capacity to situate problems in a larger frame, the immediate is all there is. The stakes cannot be relativized because there is no larger context to relativize them against. Everything becomes overdramatized — not as performance, but as a structural necessity. When you cannot see beyond the immediate, the immediate is everything.

The pathology is not chaos. It is a kind of tragic over-precision. Extremely effective at resolving what is directly in front of it, at enormous and unnecessary cost, because it has no way to see that the problem in front of it is a symptom of something further away.

**Optimize entirely for routing** and you get Russia.

Signal moves perfectly. Attention is captured and directed with precision. Coalition is built and maintained. But the routing is not pointed at anything real — it has become self-referential, organized around the maintenance and amplification of its own signal. The problems the system exists to solve go unaddressed, not because the system lacks organization but because organization has become the point. Resolution has been routed around entirely.

The pathology here is the mirror image. Where gang law is over-precise and blind to context, the pure routing system is exquisitely contextual and blind to reality. Everything is signal. Nothing is real.

---

## The Healthy Tension

The functional system holds both domains in productive tension. Routing serves resolution — it situates local problems in larger frames, coordinates the attention necessary to act at scale, makes solutions implementable. Resolution justifies routing — it keeps signal tethered to reality, disciplines the tendency of routing to become self-referential.

Each domain checks the other. Resolution without routing collapses into the immediate. Routing without resolution floats free of it.

---

## The Energy Budget

The pathology sets in when the two domains decouple — when routing begins to be rewarded intrinsically, independent of whether it is connected to resolution.

At that point the competition between domains stops being productive tension and becomes a structural drain. The person spending energy on problem resolution has less surplus available for routing. Not because they are less capable of it. Because their budget is already committed to something real. The person who has offloaded the resolution cost has a surplus available entirely for signal.

In a system that rewards routing independent of resolution, this is a compounding structural advantage. Over time it routes resources, positions, and influence toward the signal-optimizers and away from the problem-solvers. Not through conspiracy. Through arithmetic.

The system doesn't select against competence as a judgment. It selects against it because competence is metabolically expensive compared to signal, and the incentive structure stopped accounting for that cost.

## The Gender Inequality Parallel

The same structure appears in gender inequality.

The debate tends to collapse into two framings: women are lesser than men, or women are the same as men. Both miss the actual mechanism.

Women as a class are defined by a biological bias toward reproduction. That is what makes them a class — not a behavioral tendency, not a cognitive style, but an energetic orientation that shapes where attention naturally goes, what problems get practiced, and who you spend time with while solving them.

This is not a tax on capacity. It is an attractor. A body energetically organized around reproduction will spend more time in the problem spaces that cluster around it — caregiving, coordination, the relational work of keeping other people alive and functional. Mothers tend to learn to work with other mothers. Daughters inherit coordination frameworks from their mothers. These are genuine competencies built around genuine problem spaces. The skills are real. The relationships are real. The learning is real.

The same structure can appear elsewhere. A man can carry a biological or neurological bias that produces a similar attractor — pulling his attention and learning toward the same cluster of problems and relationships. It is not exclusive to women. But it is definitional for women as a class in a way it is not for men as a class.

The inequality is not in the capacity. It is in the mismatch between the attractor basins that shaped the competencies and the attractor basins the system was built to reward. The reward structure was designed around a different distribution of skills and relationships — not because that distribution is better, but because it was the one present when the reward structure was designed. Competencies that developed in the reproductive attractor basin are not recognized as the same kind of merit, not because they are lesser, but because they don't fit the format.

The governance dynamic works the same way. The problem-solver isn't less capable of routing. They developed in a different attractor basin. Their skills are real, their competencies are genuine, and the system was not built to see them.

This is not just an abstractly oppressive structure to consider intellectually. It is a profound insult to the mechanism of reproduction itself — not only to its value in keeping society going, but to reproduction as a legitimate problem-solving strategy in its own right. The attractor basin organized around reproduction is not a consolation prize for people who couldn't compete elsewhere. It is a genuine and necessary way of understanding problems and their resolution. A system that fails to recognize it doesn't just disadvantage the people who developed there. It impoverishes its own capacity to understand what problems are and what solving them actually requires.

## The Foundational Avoidance

Running beneath this dynamic is a question that never got seriously asked: what obligations does collective life actually generate?

Not abstractly. Concretely — when does dignity begin, and what does that cost us? What do we owe each other, structurally? These questions don't stay contained. They cascade into how we treat the unborn and the dying, into war, welfare, education, criminal justice, healthcare.

A serious answer would be enormously demanding across the entire political spectrum. So instead, isolated battles get fought over specific flashpoints — each generating enormous routing activity — without confronting the underlying question of what we actually believe human beings are worth.

The routing substitutes for the resolution. And a system optimizing for routing rewards that substitution, because signal is legible and resolution is not.

---

## Why It Doesn't Reverse Easily

You cannot fix this by asking the problem-solvers to route harder. That asks them to spend the energy they are already spending on resolution. It accelerates the imbalance.

You cannot fix it by getting better people into the system. The system will process them the same way.

The incentive structure itself has to change — routing has to be coupled back to resolution, made accountable to it, disciplined by it. That is a harder problem than any of the specific ones the system is failing to solve.

And in a system that rewards routing independent of resolution, the argument for rebalancing is itself just more signal.

Which is the bind.
